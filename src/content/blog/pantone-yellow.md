##### **Understanding Pantone**

Pantone is a standardized color matching system used in various industries, including graphic design, printing, and fashion. It is a universal language for color that allows designers, manufacturers, and clients to communicate and reproduce colors accurately.

Pantone colors are identified by a unique number or name and are created using a specific mix of pigments. The Pantone Matching System (PMS) is the most commonly used Pantone system, which consists of over 1,800 colors. Each color is assigned a unique number, making it easy to identify and reproduce.

Pantone colors are used in a variety of ways, including:

- **Design:** Designers use Pantone colors to choose the exact shade they want for their designs, ensuring consistency across different mediums and materials.

- **Printing:** Printers use Pantone colors to ensure that the printed product matches the intended color. Pantone colors are particularly useful in situations where color accuracy is critical, such as in branding and packaging.

- **Fashion:** Pantone colors are used in the fashion industry to create color palettes and ensure consistency across different materials and products.

Pantone Yellow is a popular color in the Pantone system. It is a bright, vibrant yellow that is often used in branding and marketing materials. Pantone Yellow is identified by the number PANTONE 116 C and is a popular choice for companies looking to create a bold, eye-catching look.

Overall, Pantone is an essential tool for anyone working with color. It provides a standardized system for identifying and reproducing colors accurately, making it easier for designers, manufacturers, and clients to communicate and collaborate effectively.
